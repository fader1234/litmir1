## Without water and without light

In «Předtucha» the scout Petr comes back from the south: by an old dam a huge army of wild men is camping, five or six thousand people. In nine days the Vlčí kníže — the Wolf Prince — will reach the walls of Naše utopie. For nine days and nine nights the whole town gets ready: they dig pits, sew a giant canvas and collect mirrors. And every day Robert walks round the town asking people whether they have enough blankets and whether the children are eating. Let’s imagine one of these conversations.

> — Dobrý den, paní Marková. **Máte dost dek?**{Do you have enough blankets?}
> — Máme, Roberte, děkuju. Ale **bez vody a bez světla**{without water and without light} dlouho nevydržíme.
> — **Vody máme dost.**{We have enough water.} Tomáš staví **u stavidla**{by the sluice} hráz.
> — **A kolik jich je?**{And how many of them are there?}
> — **Podle Petra**{According to Petr} **pět, možná šest tisíc**{five, maybe six thousand}. **Nás je čtyři sta dvacet.**{There are four hundred and twenty of us.}
> — **Odkud přijdou?**{Where will they come from?}
> — **Z jihu, od staré přehrady.**{From the south, from the old dam.} Za devět dní.
> — A proč děti nosí **zrcadla ze všech domů**{mirrors from all the houses}?
> — To je **Luisův plán**{Luis’s plan}. Zrcadla jsou na slunce.
> — A **babička paní Novotné**{Mrs Novotná’s grandmother}? Ještě kašle?
> — **Trochu.**{A little.} Ale **bez Roberta**{without Robert} by tu bylo smutno.

## 1. The genitive — singular

The genitive answers **koho? čeho?** — of whom? of what? It covers English "of", "'s", and much more: it comes after many prepositions, after quantities and after numbers from five up.

| Gender | Nominative | Genitive | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| masc. animate | Robert, muž | **Roberta**, **muže** | bez Roberta, u muže |
| masc. inanimate | hrad, stroj | **hradu**, **stroje** | do hradu |
| feminine | voda, růže, kost | **vody**, **růže**, **kosti** | bez vody |
| neuter | město, moře, náměstí | **města**, **moře**, **náměstí** | z města |

Soft stems (ending in -ž, -š, -č, -ř, -j, -c…) take **-e**: *Tomáš → Tomáše, muž → muže*, but *Petr → Petra*.

Masculine inanimate nouns mostly take **-u**: *dům → domu* (from home — **z domu**), *hrad → hradu*, *čas → času*. A few take **-a**: *les → lesa, chléb → chleba, sýr → sýra*, many town names (*od Tábora*) and months (*do května*). Learn these as you meet them.

Adjectives: *starý → **starého** hradu, stará → **staré** přehrady*, plural **-ých**: *ze starých aut*.

## 2. The genitive — plural

| Gender | Ending | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| masculine | **-ů** | divoch → **divochů**, muž → **mužů**, hrad → **hradů** |
| feminine in -a | **Ø** (no ending) | žena → **žen**, deka → **dek**, sestra → **sester** |
| feminine in -e, -ost | **-í** | růže → **růží**, kost → **kostí**, píseň → **písní** |
| neuter in -o | **Ø** | město → **měst**, okno → **oken**, auto → **aut** |
| neuter in -e, -í | **-í** | moře → **moří**, náměstí → **náměstí** |

The "zero" ending is a real ending: *pět žen* — five women, literally "five of-women". When the word would end in two consonants, an **e** often slips in: *sestra → sester, okno → oken*. Words in -ice also lose the -e: *ulice → ulic, vesnice → vesnic*.

Irregular but frequent: *lidé → **lidí***, *děti → **dětí***, *peníze → **peněz***.

## 3. Prepositions with the genitive

| Preposition | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **bez** (beze) | without | bez vody, *beze slova* |
| **do** | into, to; until | do školy, do Prahy, do pátku |
| **z** (ze) | from, out of, off | z Prahy, ze sklepa, z jihu |
| **od** | from (a person / point), since | od Plzně, krabice od sušenek |
| **u** | at, by, at someone’s place | u Roberta, u brány |
| **kolem** | around, past | kolem města |
| **podle** | according to; along | podle Petra, podle plánu |
| **místo** | instead of | místo zdi |
| **během** | during | během noci |

Traps for English speakers: **do** has nothing to do with the verb "to do" — it means "into / to": *jdu do školy* — I’m going to school. **U** is like French *chez*: *u Roberta* — at Robert’s place. And **z** vs **od**: *z* is "out of" a place (*z Prahy*), *od* is "from" a person or a starting point (*dopis od Jany* — a letter from Jana).

From the novel: *Lucie mu **beze slova** podala čutoru.* — Lucie handed him the flask without a word. Petr is *kluk s vlkem na krku a **bez vousů*** — a boy with a wolf on his neck and no beard. They voted with slips of paper in an old tin box *od sušenek* — a biscuit tin.

## 4. How many? — KOLIK + genitive

After words of quantity, use the genitive: **kolik** lidí? (how many people?), **hodně** práce (a lot of work), **málo** času (little time), **moc** zrcadel (many mirrors), **trochu** vody (a bit of water), **dost** dek (enough blankets), **víc** jídla (more food).

Numbers follow a three-step rule:

| Number | Case | Example |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | nominative singular | jeden metr, jedna noc |
| 2, 3, 4 | **nominative plural** | dva **metry**, tři **dny**, čtyři **hodiny** |
| 5 and up | **genitive plural** | pět **metrů**, devět **dní**, sto **lidí** |

Luis at the dam: *„Ještě dva metry.“* — Two more metres. Jana sleeps **čtyři hodiny** a day. But: *pět tisíc lidí*.

Hundreds and thousands: *sto, dvě stě, tři sta, **pět set***; *tisíc, dva tisíce, **pět tisíc***: *pět set divochů* — five hundred wild men. After 5+ the verb is neuter singular: ***Přišlo** jich dvacet tři.* — Twenty-three of them came (the wild men who surrendered at the gate).

## 5. Whose? Possession, negation and verbs

**Possession** uses the genitive after the noun: *dům **paní Markové*** — Mrs Marková’s house, *vlajka **Utopie*** — the flag of Utopia, *konec **války*** — the end of the war. With a single name Czechs prefer a possessive adjective: *Luisův plán, Janin tablet, Robertova židle* — in the novel: *Luisův šílený plán a **Luciiny** ruce*.

**Negation**: in everyday Czech a negative verb takes the normal accusative: ***Nemám čas.** Nemáme vodu.* You may see *nemám času* in old books — it sounds bookish, don’t copy it.

Some verbs take the genitive: **bát se** (to be afraid of): *bojím se psa*; **zeptat se** (to ask someone): ***Zeptej se Roberta.*** — Ask Robert.
