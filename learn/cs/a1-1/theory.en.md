## Meet Jana and Luis

Luis is a musician from Argentina. In a Prague café he meets Jana — and so the novel «Podzemní tajemství» begins.

Hover over a word (or tap it) to see its translation.

> — **Ahoj**{Hi}! **Já jsem**{I am} Jana. **Jak se jmenuješ?**{What’s your name?}
> — **Ahoj, Jano**{Hi, Jana}! **Jmenuji se**{My name is} Luis. **Těší mě**{Nice to meet you}.
> — **Mě taky**{Me too}. **Odkud jsi?**{Where are you from?}
> — **Jsem z Argentiny**{I’m from Argentina}. **Jsem hudebník**{I’m a musician}. **A ty?**{And you?}
> — **Jsem z Prahy**{I’m from Prague}. **Jsem Češka**{I’m Czech (female)}. **Jak se máš?**{How are you?}
> — **Dobře, děkuju**{Fine, thanks}! **Na shledanou**{Goodbye}!
> — **Čau**{Bye}, Luisi!

## 1. Greetings and goodbyes

| Čeština | English |
|---|---|
| Ahoj | Hi / Bye (informal) |
| Dobrý den | Hello, good day (formal) |
| Dobré ráno | Good morning |
| Dobrý večer | Good evening |
| Dobrou noc | Good night |
| Na shledanou | Goodbye |
| Čau | Bye |
| Děkuju / Prosím | Thank you / Please, you’re welcome |

**Dobrý den** is the standard polite "hello" all day long. **Prosím** means "please", "you’re welcome" and "pardon?".

## 2. Introducing yourself

- **Jak se jmenuješ? / Jak se jmenujete?** — What’s your name? (informal / formal)
- **Jmenuji se Jana. / Já jsem Jana.** — My name is Jana. / I’m Jana.
- **Těší mě.** — Nice to meet you.
- **Jak se máš? — Dobře.** — How are you? — Fine.

When you address someone, the name changes (the vocative case): *Jana → **Jano**, Luis → **Luisi***.

## 3. Subject pronouns

| Čeština | English |
|---|---|
| já | I |
| ty | you (informal) |
| on / ona / ono | he / she / it |
| my | we |
| vy | you (plural, formal) |
| oni / ony | they |

The pronoun is usually dropped: **Jsem z Prahy** = "I’m from Prague".

## 4. The verb “být”

As in English, Czech needs the verb "to be": *Já **jsem** Jana* — "I am Jana".

| Person | BÝT | Example |
|---|---|---|
| já | **jsem** | Já jsem Jana. |
| ty | **jsi** | Jsi hudebník? |
| on / ona | **je** | Luis je z Argentiny. |
| my | **jsme** | Jsme přátelé. |
| vy | **jste** | Jste z Ruska? |
| oni | **jsou** | Jana a Lucie jsou z Prahy. |

The negative is written as one word: **nejsem, nejsi, není, nejsme, nejste, nejsou**. *Nejsem Čech.* — I’m not Czech.

## 5. Where are you from?

- **Odkud jsi? / Odkud jste?** — Where are you from?
- **Jsem z Prahy / z Moskvy.** — I’m from Prague / from Moscow.
- **Jsem z Ruska / z Argentiny.** — I’m from Russia / from Argentina.

After **z** ("from") the noun changes its ending: *Praha → z Prahy, Rusko → z Ruska*. Nationalities are capitalised:

| He | She | English |
|---|---|---|
| Čech | Češka | Czech |
| Rus | Ruska | Russian |
| Argentinec | Argentinka | Argentinian |
| Ital | Italka | Italian |
| Španěl | Španělka | Spanish |
| Angličan | Angličanka | English |

## 6. Pronunciation

| Letters | Sound | Example |
|---|---|---|
| č, š, ž | "ch", "sh", "zh" | čau, Češka |
| ř | a rolled "r" + "zh" together | řeka, Dvořák |
| c | "ts" | cesta |
| ch | "kh", as in *loch* | chleba |
| ě | "ye" | děkuju |
| á, é, í, ó, ú, ů, ý | long vowels | dobrý den, máš |
| h | a voiced "h" | Praha |

## 7. Good to know

The stress in Czech is **always on the first syllable**: *DĚ-ku-ju, NA-shle-da-nou*. Vowel length (á, í…) has nothing to do with stress.
