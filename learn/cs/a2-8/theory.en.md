## Kdybys tu závoru neotevřela…

In «Předtucha», on the evening of the day they bury the dead after the battle, Robert and Lucie meet on the walls — where they always meet. Lucie opened the sluice gate at the dam and almost died doing it. Robert tells her something he has been carrying for a long time (chapter 30). Here is a simplified scene — it is full of "would" and "if".

> — **Lucie. Na hrázi. To, co jsi udělala.**{Lucie. At the dam. What you did.}
> — **Neříkej to.**{Don’t say it.}
> — **Musím. Kdybys tu závoru neotevřela**{I have to. If you hadn’t opened that gate}, **nikdo z nás by tu dneska nestál.**{none of us would be standing here today.}
> — **A kdyby mě ta voda vzala?**{And if the water had taken me?} **Co by se stalo, Roberte?**{What would have happened, Robert?}
> — **Neodpustil bych si to.**{I would never forgive myself.} **Ztratil jsem jednu ženu.**{I lost one woman.} **Nechci ztratit druhou.**{I don’t want to lose another.}
> — **Druhou?**{Another?}
> — …
> — **Natálie by tě měla ráda.**{Natálie would have liked you.}

## 1. The conditional: by + the -l form

English "would" never changes. The Czech "would" **changes with the person**. It is built on the same **-l** form as the past tense:

| | Czech | English |
|---|---|---|
| já | dělal / dělala **bych** | I would do |
| ty | dělal / dělala **bys** | you would do |
| on / ona / ono | dělal / dělala / dělalo **by** | he / she / it would do |
| my | dělali **bychom** | we would do |
| vy | dělali **byste** | you would do |
| oni | dělali **by** | they would do |

Compare the past tense: *dělal **jsem*** → *dělal **bych***. *Bych* simply takes the place of *jsem*, and like *jsem* it goes in **second position** in the sentence: *Neodpustil **bych** si to. To **bych** nikdy neudělal.* — I’d never do that.

In speech you will often hear **bysme** instead of *bychom*; in writing use *bychom*.

With reflexive *se* and "ty": **bys + se = by ses**. Robert to Martin, the young officer: *«Měl **by ses** vyspat.»* — You should get some sleep (chapter 35).

## 2. Being polite: chtěl bych, mohl byste

| Czech | English |
|---|---|
| **Chtěl / Chtěla bych** kávu. | I would like a coffee. |
| **Dal / Dala bych si** čaj. | I’d have a tea. |
| **Mohl / Mohla byste** mi pomoct? | Could you help me? (polite) |
| **Měl bys** spát. | You should sleep. |
| **Bylo by** hezké… | It would be nice… |

Robert’s letter to Natálie is full of them: *Hana… malá a přísná, **líbila by se ti**.* — Hana… small and strict, you’d like her. *Hana **by mi vynadala**.* — Hana would tell me off. *Ty **bys udělala** totéž.* — You would have done the same.

## 3. KDYBY — "if" for things that are not real

**Kdyby** changes too: **kdybych, kdybys, kdyby, kdybychom, kdybyste, kdyby** + the -l form. The other half of the sentence is an ordinary conditional. Unlike English, Czech uses no special "if I had" tense — both halves have the same form:

*Kdybych měl čas, šel bych s tebou.* — If I had time, I would go with you.
*Kdybys přišla, byl bych rád.* — If you came, I’d be glad.

Careful: *kdybych* already contains *bych*. Never say *~~kdyby bych~~* or *~~kdyby jsme~~*!

On the last night in the mill, Lucie asks Robert (chapter 35):

*«**Kdyby** zítra všechno **dopadlo** dobře. **Kdybychom přežili.** **Kdyby** Mocnost **odešla**. **Měla bych** u tebe místo?»* — If everything turned out well tomorrow. If we survived. If the Power left. Would there be a place for me with you?

Robert answers with a real condition — **jestli** + a normal tense: *«**Jestli** zítra **přežijeme**, chci se to naučit.»* — If we survive tomorrow, I want to learn it. **Jestli / když** = a real "if", **kdyby** = "if… would".

## 4. ABY — "so that", "to", "want someone to"

**Aby** changes as well: **abych, abys, aby, abychom, abyste, aby** + the -l form. Use it where English says "want *you* to…" or "so that…":

| Example | English |
|---|---|
| *Nechci, **abys** ji **zapomněl**.* (Lucie, ch. 22) | I don’t want you to forget her. |
| *Chci, **abys** mi **odpověděl** upřímně.* (ch. 35) | I want you to answer me honestly. |
| *Mrtví by chtěli, **abychom žili**.* (Robert’s letter) | The dead would want us to live. |
| *Sešla do sklepa, **aby** dětem **řekla**, že je ráno.* (Jana, ch. 26) | She went down to the cellar to tell the children it was morning. |

Never *~~Chci tě odpovědět~~*: "want someone to" is always *chci, abys…*. With the same subject, use the infinitive: *Chci odpovědět.* — I want to answer. With *se*: **abys + se = aby ses**: *Chtěla jsem, **aby ses** tak díval na mě.* — I wanted you to look at me like that (Jana, ch. 35).

## 5. The unreal past — briefly

In everyday Czech the same form also covers the past: *Kdybys tu závoru **neotevřela**, nikdo by tu **nestál**.* — If you hadn’t opened the gate, nobody would be standing here. Hence the unit title: **Kdybych tě neztratil…** — If I hadn’t lost you… There is also a bookish past form, *byl bych udělal* ("I would have done"), but you only need to recognise it.
