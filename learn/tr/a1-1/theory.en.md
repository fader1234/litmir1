## Meet Robert and Rosa

The novel «Çöl Gülü»: after the Great Catastrophe, Robert searches the desert for survivors. Wounded, he wakes up in a rebel camp — and meets Rosa.

Hover over a word (or tap it) to see its translation.

> — **Merhaba**{Hello}! **Benim adım**{My name is} Rosa. **Senin adın ne?**{What’s your name?}
> — **Merhaba**{Hello} Rosa. **Ben**{I am} Robert. **Memnun oldum**{Nice to meet you}.
> — **Ben de memnun oldum**{Nice to meet you too}. **Nasılsın?**{How are you?}
> — **İyiyim**{I’m fine}, **teşekkürler**{thank you}. **Sen**{And you} **nasılsın?**{how are you?}
> — **Ben de iyiyim**{I’m fine too}. **Burası bir kamp**{This is a camp}. **Biz özgürüz**{We are free}.
> — **Görüşürüz**{See you}, Rosa!

## 2. Introducing yourself

- **Adın ne?** — What’s your name?
- **Benim adım Rosa. / Ben Rosa.** — My name is Rosa. / I’m Rosa.
- **Memnun oldum.** — Nice to meet you.
- **Nasılsın? — İyiyim.** — How are you? — I’m fine.

## 3. Subject pronouns

| Türkçe | English |
|---|---|
| ben | I |
| sen | you (informal) |
| o | he, she, it |
| biz | we |
| siz | you (plural, formal) |
| onlar | they |

Turkish has no gender: **o** means "he", "she" and "it". Pronouns are often dropped, because the ending already shows the person.

## 4. The verb “olmak”

In Turkish, "to be" is an **ending** glued onto the word: *öğrenci* (student) → *öğrenci**yim*** (I’m a student). There is no separate verb like English "am".

| Person | OLMAK | Example |
|---|---|---|
| ben | -(y)ım / -(y)im | Ben öğrenciyim. |
| sen | -sın / -sin | Sen öğrencisin. |
| o | — (-dır) | O öğrenci. |
| biz | -(y)ız / -(y)iz | Biz öğrenciyiz. |
| siz | -sınız / -siniz | Siz öğrencisiniz. |
| onlar | -lar / -ler | Onlar öğrenciler. |

**Vowel harmony**: the vowel of the ending matches the last vowel of the word. After *a, ı* — **ı**, after *e, i* — **i**, after *o, u* — **u**, after *ö, ü* — **ü**: *Rus**um*** (I’m Russian), *Türk**üm*** (I’m Turkish), *iyi**yim*** (I’m fine). A **y** is added when the word ends in a vowel.

## 5. Where are you from?

- **Nerelisin?** — Where are you from?
- **Moskova’lıyım. / İstanbul’luyum.** — I’m from Moscow. / I’m from Istanbul.
- **Rusya’dan geliyorum.** — I come from Russia.

The suffix **-lı / -li / -lu / -lü** means "from (a place)": *İstanbul**lu***, *Moskova**lı***. Nationalities don’t change for gender:

| He | She | English |
|---|---|---|
| Türk | Türk | Turkish |
| Rus | Rus | Russian |
| Arjantinli | Arjantinli | Argentinian |
| İtalyan | İtalyan | Italian |
| İspanyol | İspanyol | Spanish |
| İngiliz | İngiliz | English |

## 6. Pronunciation

| Letters | Sound | Example |
|---|---|---|
| c | "j" as in *jam* | cevap |
| ç | "ch" | çöl |
| ş | "sh" | teşekkürler |
| ı (без точки) | a short "uh" | nasılsın |
| ğ | lengthens the previous vowel | dağ |
| ö, ü | like German ö, ü | Görüşürüz |
| İ, i / I, ı | different letters: "ee" / "uh" | İstanbul, Işık |

## 7. Good to know

Turkish uses the Latin alphabet, and almost every letter always sounds the same. The stress is usually on the last syllable; *mer**ha**ba* is an exception.
