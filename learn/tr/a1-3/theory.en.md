## Where is Rosa?

Early morning in the camp. Robert can’t find Rosa anywhere, so he asks Ayşe, the old woman who bakes bread for everyone.

> — **Günaydın**{Good morning}, Ayşe Teyze! **Rosa nerede?**{Where is Rosa?}
> — **Rosa kuyuda**{Rosa is at the well}. **Su alıyor**{She is getting water}.
> — **Ya çocuklar?**{And the children?} **Onlar nerede?**{Where are they?}
> — **Çocuklar okulda**{The children are at school}. **Ders çalışıyorlar**{They are studying}.
> — **Siz ne yapıyorsunuz?**{What are you doing?}
> — **Ekmek yapıyorum**{I’m making bread}. **Sen nereye gidiyorsun?**{Where are you going?}
> — **Kuyuya gidiyorum**{I’m going to the well}. **Rosa’yı arıyorum**{I’m looking for Rosa}.
> — **Acele et**{Hurry up}, oğlum. **Rosa seni bekliyor**{Rosa is waiting for you}.

## 1. Where? The ending -DA / -DE

"In, at, on" is an **ending** in Turkish. It has two vowels (like the plural) and two consonants:

| After | Last vowel a, ı, o, u | Last vowel e, i, ö, ü |
|---|---|---|
| most sounds | **-da**: kuyu**da**, okul**da**, çadır**da** | **-de**: ev**de**, çöl**de** |
| ç, f, h, k, p, s, ş, t | **-ta**: kamp**ta**, sokak**ta** | **-te**: market**te** |

- **Nerede?** — Where? → *Rosa kuyu**da**.* — Rosa is at the well.
- **burada / şurada / orada** — here / there / over there.
- With names, use an apostrophe: *İstanbul**’da**, Ankara**’da***.

The ending can take the personal "to be" from unit 1: *Ben evde**yim**.* — I’m at home. *Sen neredesin?* — Where are you?

## 2. Where to? The ending -(Y)A / -(Y)E

"To" a place is also an ending: **-a / -e** (after a vowel, **-ya / -ye**):

| Türkçe | English |
|---|---|
| **Nereye?** | Where to? |
| okul**a**, kamp**a** | to school, to the camp |
| ev**e** | home |
| kuyu**ya** | to the well |
| İstanbul**’a** | to Istanbul |

## 3. The present continuous: -(I)YOR

This is the tense for "I am doing (now)" — and very often for everyday actions too. Take the verb, drop **-mak / -mek**, and add **-ıyor / -iyor / -uyor / -üyor** (four-way harmony) plus the personal ending:

| | yapmak (to do, make) | gelmek (to come) |
|---|---|---|
| ben | yap**ıyorum** | gel**iyorum** |
| sen | yap**ıyorsun** | gel**iyorsun** |
| o | yap**ıyor** | gel**iyor** |
| biz | yap**ıyoruz** | gel**iyoruz** |
| siz | yap**ıyorsunuz** | gel**iyorsunuz** |
| onlar | yap**ıyorlar** | gel**iyorlar** |

The **-yor** part never changes. Only the vowel before it follows harmony: *gül**ü**yor* (is laughing), *otur**u**yor* (is sitting).

**After a vowel:** stems ending in a vowel just add **-yor**, and a final **a / e** turns into **ı / i**: *oku → oku**yor***, *ara → ar**ı**yor*, *bekle → bekl**i**yor*.

**Special ones:** *gitmek → **gid**iyorum* (t → d), *etmek → **ed**iyorum*, *yemek → **yi**yorum* (to eat), *demek → **di**yorum* (to say).

## 4. Not doing it, and questions

- **Negative**: add **-m-** before the vowel: *yap**m**ıyorum* — I’m not doing; *gel**m**iyor* — he isn’t coming.
- **Question**: **mu** written separately, with the personal ending on it: *Geliyor **musun**?* — Are you coming? *Rosa uyuyor **mu**?* — Is Rosa sleeping?

The question word after **-yor** is always **mu**, because the last vowel is **o**.
