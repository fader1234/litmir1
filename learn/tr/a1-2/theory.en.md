## Rosa shows Robert the camp

Robert can walk again. Rosa takes him around the rebel camp in the desert: tents, a well, people he has never seen before.

Hover over a word (or tap it) to see its translation.

> — Rosa, **bu ne?**{what is this?}
> — **Bu bir çadır**{This is a tent}. **Burası bizim kampımız**{This is our camp}.
> — **Şu ne?**{What is that?}
> — **Şu bir kuyu**{That is a well}. **Kampta su var**{There is water in the camp}, **ama elektrik yok**{but there is no electricity}.
> — **Onlar kim?**{Who are they?}
> — **Onlar asiler**{They are rebels}. **Benim arkadaşlarım**{My friends}.
> — **Onlar asker mi?**{Are they soldiers?}
> — **Hayır, asker değiller**{No, they aren’t soldiers}. **Öğretmenler, doktorlar, öğrenciler**{Teachers, doctors, students}…
> — **Kampta kaç kişi var?**{How many people are there in the camp?}
> — **Kırk iki**{Forty-two}. **Bir de köpek**{And a dog}!

## 1. This, that: BU, ŞU, O

| Türkçe | English |
|---|---|
| **bu** | this (here, near me) |
| **şu** | that (there, near you, or something I point at) |
| **o** | that (over there, far away) |

- **Bu ne?** — What is this? → **Bu bir çadır.** — This is a tent.
- **Bu kim?** — Who is this? → **Bu Rosa.** — This is Rosa.
- **bir** = "a / an" (and also "one"): *bir çadır* — a tent.

Plural: **bunlar** (these), **şunlar**, **onlar** (those; also "they").

## 2. Plural: -LAR / -LER

Turkish has a plural ending with two forms. Look at the **last vowel** of the word:

| Last vowel | Ending | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| a, ı, o, u | **-lar** | çadır → çadır**lar**, kitap → kitap**lar**, okul → okul**lar** |
| e, i, ö, ü | **-ler** | ev → ev**ler**, öğrenci → öğrenci**ler**, göz → göz**ler** |

**Important:** after a number the noun stays **singular**: *iki çadır* (two tents), *kırk kişi* (forty people) — never *iki çadırlar*.

## 3. Questions: MI / Mİ / MU / MÜ

A yes–no question is made with the little word **mı**, written **separately**. Its vowel follows the four-way harmony you know from unit 1:

| Last vowel | Question word | Example |
|---|---|---|
| a, ı | **mı** | Asker **mı**? |
| e, i | **mi** | Öğrenci **mi**? |
| o, u | **mu** | Doktor **mu**? |
| ö, ü | **mü** | Türk **mü**? |

The personal ending goes onto **mı**, not onto the noun:

| | |
|---|---|
| Öğrenci **miyim**? | Am I a student? |
| Öğrenci **misin**? | Are you a student? |
| Öğrenci **mi**? | Is he/she a student? |
| Öğrenci **miyiz**? | Are we students? |
| Öğrenci **misiniz**? | Are you (pl.) students? |
| Öğrenci**ler mi**? | Are they students? |

Answer: **Evet** (yes) / **Hayır** (no).

## 4. Not: DEĞİL

To say "is not", use **değil** after the noun and add the personal ending to it:

| | |
|---|---|
| Asker **değilim**. | I’m not a soldier. |
| Asker **değilsin**. | You aren’t a soldier. |
| Asker **değil**. | He/she isn’t a soldier. |
| Asker **değiliz**. | We aren’t soldiers. |
| Asker **değilsiniz**. | You (pl.) aren’t soldiers. |
| Asker **değiller**. | They aren’t soldiers. |

## 5. There is / there isn’t: VAR and YOK

| Türkçe | English |
|---|---|
| Kampta su **var**. | There is water in the camp. |
| Kampta elektrik **yok**. | There is no electricity in the camp. |
| Su **var mı**? — **Var.** / **Yok.** | Is there water? — Yes. / No. |
| Benim bir köpeğim **var**. | I have a dog. |

**Var** and **yok** are also how Turkish says "to have" (you will learn this properly later).

## 6. Numbers

| | | | | | |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | sıfır | 6 | altı | 20 | yirmi |
| 1 | bir | 7 | yedi | 30 | otuz |
| 2 | iki | 8 | sekiz | 40 | kırk |
| 3 | üç | 9 | dokuz | 50 | elli |
| 4 | dört | 10 | on | 60 | altmış |
| 5 | beş | 11 | on bir | 100 | yüz |

Numbers simply join: *on iki* (12), *kırk iki* (42), *yüz yirmi beş* (125). **Kaç?** — How many? → *Kaç kişi?* — How many people? (with a singular noun!)
