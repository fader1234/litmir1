## Meet Marco, the detective from Sinea

This is how the novel «Il treno fantasma» begins. Read it — you already understand almost everything:

> **Mi chiamo Marco.** **Ho 35 anni**{I’m 35 years old}. **Sono un detective**{I’m a detective}. **Vivo**{I live} a Sinea. Sinea è una bella città.

Now let’s imagine that on the night train Marco meets Laura, the art collector from Florence.

> — Buonasera. **Che lavoro fai?**{What do you do for a living?}
> — **Faccio il detective**{I work as a detective}. **Lavoro**{I work} a Sinea. E tu?
> — **Sono collezionista**{I’m a collector}. **Compro**{I buy} quadri e **vivo**{I live} in una villa **vicino a**{near} Firenze.
> — **Parli**{You speak} molto bene! **Di dove sei?**{Where are you from?}
> — Sono di Firenze. **Non parlo**{I don’t speak} inglese, ma **capisco**{I understand} lo spagnolo. E tu, **che cosa cerchi**{what are you looking for} su questo treno?
> — **Cerco**{I’m looking for} **la mia ragazza**{my girlfriend}, Alice.

## 1. What do you do?

- **Che lavoro fai?** — What do you do? (literally: "what work do you do?")
- **Sono un detective. / Faccio il detective.** — I’m a detective.
- **Lavoro come** cameriere. — I work as a waiter.

After *essere* the article before a profession is optional (*Sono un detective* / *Sono detective*). After *fare* you need the definite article: ***Faccio il** medico.*

## 3. Present tense: regular verbs

All Italian verbs end in **-are**, **-ere** or **-ire** in the dictionary:

| | parlare (to speak) | vivere (to live) | dormire (to sleep) |
|---|---|---|---|
| io | parl**o** | viv**o** | dorm**o** |
| tu | parl**i** | viv**i** | dorm**i** |
| lui / lei / Lei | parl**a** | viv**e** | dorm**e** |
| noi | parl**iamo** | viv**iamo** | dorm**iamo** |
| voi | parl**ate** | viv**ete** | dorm**ite** |
| loro | parl**ano** | viv**ono** | dorm**ono** |

The *io, tu, noi* forms are the same in all three groups: **-o, -i, -iamo**. In the *loro* form the stress stays on the stem: *PAR-la-no, VI-vo-no*.

Many *-ire* verbs add **-isc-**: **capire** (to understand) — *cap**isc**o, cap**isc**i, cap**isc**e, capiamo, capite, cap**isc**ono*. Also: *finire* (to finish), *preferire* (to prefer).

## 4. Verbs in this unit

| -are | -ere | -ire |
|---|---|---|
| lavorare — to work | vivere — to live | dormire — to sleep |
| parlare — to speak | leggere — to read | partire — to leave |
| cercare — to look for | scrivere — to write | capire (-isc-) — to understand |
| comprare — to buy | prendere — to take | finire (-isc-) — to finish |
| abitare — to live, reside | vedere — to see | aprire — to open |

Verbs in **-care / -gare** add an **h** before *i* to keep the hard sound: *cercare → tu cerc**h**i, noi cerc**h**iamo*.

## 5. Negation and questions

- Negation is **non** before the verb: ***Non** parlo inglese.* — I don’t speak English.
- A question differs from a statement only in intonation: *Parli italiano.* → ***Parli italiano?***
- Question words: **che cosa? / cosa?** — what? · **dove?** — where? · **chi?** — who? · **come?** — how? · **quanti?** — how many?

## 6. How old are you?

- **Quanti anni hai?** — How old are you?
- **Ho 35 (trentacinque) anni.** — I’m 35. Literally "I have 35 years" — the verb **avere** (to have); its full conjugation is in unit 3.

| 0 zero | 1 uno | 2 due | 3 tre | 4 quattro | 5 cinque |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 6 sei | 7 sette | 8 otto | 9 nove | 10 dieci | 11 undici |
| 12 dodici | 15 quindici | 20 venti | 21 ventuno | 28 ventotto | 30 trenta |
| 35 trentacinque | 40 quaranta | 50 cinquanta | 82 ottantadue | 100 cento | 1000 mille |

Before *uno* and *otto* the tens drop their final vowel: *vent**uno**, vent**otto**, trent**uno***.
