## Meet Alessio and Laura

Alessio lives in Florence and loves art. Laura Bellini is a wealthy collector. They are the heroes of the novel «Ladro di cuori». One evening, at an exhibition, they meet.

Hover over a word (or tap it) to see its translation.

> — **Buonasera**{Good evening}! **Mi chiamo**{My name is} Alessio. **E tu, come ti chiami?**{And what’s your name?}
> — Mi chiamo Laura. Laura Bellini. **Piacere**{Nice to meet you}.
> — **Piacere mio**{The pleasure is mine}. **Di dove sei?**{Where are you from?}
> — **Sono di**{I’m from} Firenze. **Sono italiana**{I’m Italian}. **E tu?**{And you?}
> — **Anch’io**{Me too} sono di Firenze. **Sono un artista**{I’m an artist}… più o meno.
> — **Come stai?**{How are you?}
> — **Benissimo**{Very well}, **grazie**{thank you}. **Arrivederci**{Goodbye}, Laura!

## 1. Greetings and goodbyes

| Italiano | English |
|---|---|
| Ciao | Hi / Bye (informal) |
| Buongiorno | Good morning |
| Buonasera | Good evening |
| Buonanotte | Good night |
| Arrivederci | Goodbye |
| A presto | See you soon |
| Grazie / Prego | Thank you / You’re welcome |

**Ciao** means both "hi" and "bye", but only with friends. With strangers, say **Buongiorno** or **Buonasera**.

## 2. Introducing yourself

- **Come ti chiami?** — What’s your name?
- **Mi chiamo Alessio.** — My name is Alessio.
- **Sono Laura.** — I’m Laura.
- **Piacere!** — Nice to meet you!
- **Come si chiama? (Lei)** — What’s your name? (formal)

## 3. Subject pronouns

| Italiano | English |
|---|---|
| io | I |
| tu | you (informal) |
| lui / lei | he / she |
| Lei | you (formal) |
| noi | we |
| voi | you (plural) |
| loro | they |

The formal **Lei** ("you") is capitalised and takes the same verb form as *lei* (she). As in Spanish, the pronoun is usually dropped: **Sono di Firenze** = "I’m from Florence".

## 4. The verb “essere”

| Person | ESSERE | Example |
|---|---|---|
| io | **sono** | Io sono Alessio. |
| tu | **sei** | Tu sei italiana? |
| lui / lei / Lei | **è** | Laura è di Firenze. |
| noi | **siamo** | Siamo amici. |
| voi | **siete** | Siete di Roma? |
| loro | **sono** | Alessio e Laura sono italiani. |

**È** (is) has an accent, **e** (and) doesn’t: *Laura **è** bella **e** ricca.* The forms *io sono* and *loro sono* are the same.

## 5. Where are you from?

- **Di dove sei?** — Where are you from?
- **Sono di Firenze / di Mosca.** — I’m from Florence / from Moscow (city).
- **Sono dell’Argentina / della Russia.** — I’m from Argentina / from Russia (country).

With a city use **di**, with a country **di + article** (*dell’, della*). Nationalities are written in lower case and change for gender: **-o → -a**; words in **-e** don’t change.

| He | She | English |
|---|---|---|
| italiano | italiana | Italian |
| russo | russa | Russian |
| argentino | argentina | Argentinian |
| spagnolo | spagnola | Spanish |
| inglese | inglese | English |
| francese | francese | French |

## 6. Pronunciation

| Letters | Sound | Example |
|---|---|---|
| c + e, i | "ch" as in *church* | ciao, Firenze → «ча́о» |
| ch + e, i | "k" | chiamo, che |
| g + e, i | "j" as in *jam* | Gelato, Giulia |
| gn | "ny" as in *canyon* | spagnolo, bagno |
| gli | soft "ly" | figlio, famiglia |
| sc + e, i | "sh" | scena, uscire |
| h | silent | ho, hai |

## 7. Good to know

Double consonants are pronounced long: **nonno** (grandfather) ≠ **nono** (ninth). The stress usually falls on the second-to-last syllable; if it falls on the last, it’s written: *città, perché*.
