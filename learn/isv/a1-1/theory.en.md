## Meet Robert and Kennet

Interslavic is a language that speakers of any Slavic language understand without study. In the novel «Prědčutje» Robert rides through a dead city and meets a survivor, Kennet.

Hover over a word (or tap it) to see its translation.

> — **Zdravo**{Hi}! **Ja jesm**{I am} Robert. **Kako se zoveš?**{What’s your name?}
> — **Moje ime jest**{My name is} Kennet.
> — **Odkud jesi, Kennete?**{Where are you from, Kennet?}
> — **Iz daleka**{From far away}. **A ty?**{And you?}
> — **Ja jesm iz**{I’m from} bunkera. **Dva lěta**{Two years} pod zemjeju.
> — **Kako jesi?**{How are you?}
> — **Dobro**{Fine}, **hvala**{thanks}. **Dobrodošli**{Welcome}!

## 1. Greetings and goodbyes

| Medžuslovjansky | English |
|---|---|
| Zdravo! / Dobry denj! | Hi! / Good day! |
| Dobro jutro | Good morning |
| Dobry večer | Good evening |
| Dobru noć | Good night |
| Do viděnja | Goodbye |
| Hvala | Thank you |
| Prošu | Please |
| Dobrodošli! | Welcome! |

The words are chosen so that every Slav recognises them: *hvala* as in Serbian and Croatian, *dobry denj* as in Russian and Czech.

## 2. Introducing yourself

- **Kako se zoveš? / Kako jest tvoje ime?** — What’s your name?
- **Moje ime jest Kennet. / Ja jesm Robert.** — My name is Kennet. / I’m Robert.
- **Kako jesi? — Dobro.** — How are you? — Fine.

As in Serbian and Czech, a name changes when you address someone: *Kennet → **Kennete***, *Saša → **Sašo***. Just like in the novel: «Odkud jesi, Kennete?»

## 3. Subject pronouns

| Medžuslovjansky | English |
|---|---|
| ja | I |
| ty | you (informal) |
| on / ona / ono | he / she / it |
| my | we |
| vy | you (plural, formal) |
| oni | they |

## 4. The verb “byti”

Interslavic uses "to be" in the present tense, as Czech and Serbian do: *Ja **jesm** Robert*. The forms look like Old Church Slavonic: «есмь, еси, есть».

| Person | BYTI | Example |
|---|---|---|
| ja | **jesm** | Ja jesm Robert. |
| ty | **jesi** | Odkud jesi? |
| on / ona | **jest (je)** | Kennet jest moj drug. |
| my | **jesmo** | My jesmo prijatelji. |
| vy | **jeste** | Vy jeste iz Rusije? |
| oni | **sut** | Oni sut v utopiji. |

Negation is **ne**: *Ja ne jesm Rus.* — I’m not Russian.

## 5. Where are you from?

- **Odkud jesi? / Odkud jeste?** — Where are you from?
- **Jesm iz Rusije / iz Moskvy.** — I’m from Russia / from Moscow.
- **Živu v Pragě.** — I live in Prague.

After **iz** ("from") the noun takes the genitive ending, as in Russian: *Moskva → iz Moskvy*.



## 6. Pronunciation

| Letters | Sound | Example |
|---|---|---|
| č, š, ž | "ch", "sh", "zh" | Prědčutje, Saša |
| c | "ts" | ulica |
| j | "y" | jesm, moje |
| ě | "e" or "ye", as in your own language | lěto, viděnja |
| h | "kh" / "h" | hvala |
| y | like "i" | dobry, byti |

## 7. Good to know

Interslavic can also be written in **Cyrillic**: *Здраво! Моје име јест Кеннет.* The novels on this site use the Latin alphabet. The language’s key idea is **intelligibility**: when in doubt, pick the most pan-Slavic word.
