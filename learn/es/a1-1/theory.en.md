## Meet Natalia and Luis

Natalia is a cardiologist from Moscow. Luis is an Argentinian from Mendoza. They are the heroes of the novel «El misterio de los Fader», and in this unit they meet each other — and you meet them too.

Hover over a word (or tap it) to see its translation.

> — ¡**Hola**{Hi}! **Me llamo**{My name is} Luis. **¿Cómo te llamas?**{What's your name?}
> — Hola, Luis. Me llamo Natalia. **Mucho gusto**{Nice to meet you}.
> — **Encantado**{Pleased to meet you (said by a man)}. **¿De dónde eres?**{Where are you from?}
> — **Soy de**{I'm from} Moscú. **Soy rusa**{I'm Russian}. **¿Y tú?**{And you?}
> — **Yo**{I} soy de Mendoza. Soy **argentino**{Argentinian}.
> — **¿Cómo estás?**{How are you?}
> — **Muy bien**{Very well}, **gracias**{thank you}. ¿Y tú?
> — **Bien**{Fine}. ¡**Hasta luego**{See you}!
> — ¡**Chau**{Bye}!

## 1. Greetings and goodbyes

| Español | English | When |
|---|---|---|
| Hola | Hi / Hello | any time, with anyone |
| Buenos días | Good morning | until lunch (around 2 p.m.) |
| Buenas tardes | Good afternoon | after lunch until dark |
| Buenas noches | Good evening / Good night | in the evening and at night |
| Adiós | Goodbye | when leaving |
| Hasta luego | See you later | the everyday "see you" |
| Hasta mañana | See you tomorrow | if you meet again tomorrow |
| Chau | Bye | the usual goodbye in Argentina |

Note: in Spain the "morning" lasts until lunch, not until noon. At 1 p.m. people still say **Buenos días**.

## 2. Introducing yourself

- **¿Cómo te llamas?** — What's your name?
- **Me llamo Natalia.** — My name is Natalia. (Literally: "I call myself Natalia".)
- **Soy Natalia.** — I'm Natalia. (This works too.)
- **Mucho gusto.** — Nice to meet you.
- **Encantado.** / **Encantada.** — Pleased to meet you. A man says *encantado*, a woman says *encantada*.
- **¿Y tú?** — And you?

## 3. Subject pronouns

| Español | English |
|---|---|
| yo | I |
| tú | you (informal, one person) |
| él / ella | he / she |
| usted | you (formal, one person) |
| nosotros / nosotras | we (*nosotras* if everyone is female) |
| vosotros / vosotras | you all (informal, Spain) |
| ellos / ellas | they (*ellas* if everyone is female) |
| ustedes | you all (formal; in Latin America, always) |

Spanish often drops the pronoun, because the verb ending already shows who is speaking. **Soy de Moscú** = "I'm from Moscow", with no *yo*.

## 4. The verb SER — "to be"

| Person | SER | Example |
|---|---|---|
| yo | **soy** | Yo soy Natalia. |
| tú | **eres** | ¿Eres argentino? |
| él / ella / usted | **es** | Luis es de Mendoza. |
| nosotros / nosotras | **somos** | Somos amigos. |
| vosotros / vosotras | **sois** | Sois de Moscú. |
| ellos / ellas / ustedes | **son** | Natalia y Tatiana son rusas. |

*Usted* takes the same form as *él / ella*: **Usted es** muy amable — You are very kind.

## 5. Where are you from? Nationalities

- **¿De dónde eres?** — Where are you from?
- **Soy de** Moscú / de Rusia / de Mendoza / de Argentina.

Nationalities change for gender and number, like adjectives:

| He | She | They |
|---|---|---|
| ruso | rusa | rusos / rusas |
| argentino | argentina | argentinos / argentinas |
| italiano | italiana | italianos / italianas |
| español | española | españoles / españolas |
| inglés | inglesa | ingleses / inglesas |

**Natalia es rusa. Luis es argentino. Sergio, Luis's father, también es argentino.**

Unlike in English, nationalities are written in lower case: *ruso*, not *Ruso*.

## 6. How are you?

- **¿Cómo estás?** — How are you? (informal)
- **¿Cómo está usted?** — How are you? (formal)
- **Muy bien** — very well · **Bien** — fine · **Más o menos** — so-so · **Mal** — bad
- **Gracias** — thank you · **De nada** — you're welcome

We will study the verb *estar* ("to be" for states and places) in a later unit. For now, learn the phrase as a whole.

## 7. The signs ¿ ? and ¡ !

Spanish marks questions and exclamations **at the beginning and at the end**: **¿Cómo te llamas?** · **¡Hola!** The upside-down sign tells you how to read the sentence before you reach the end.

## 8. Pronunciation: the essentials

| Letters | Sound | Example |
|---|---|---|
| h | silent | **h**ola — "ola" |
| ñ | "ny" as in *canyon* | espa**ñ**ol |
| j | a strong "h", as in *loch* | **J**uan |
| ll, y | "y" as in *yes* (in Argentina, "sh/zh") | me **ll**amo |
| z, ce, ci | "th" in Spain, "s" in Latin America | gra**ci**as |
| rr | rolled "r" | pe**rr**o |

The stress usually falls on the second-to-last syllable. When it doesn't, a written accent shows where it goes: Mosc**ú**, ingl**é**s.

## 9. Argentinian Spanish: "vos"

The characters in our novels are Argentinian, and instead of *tú* they say **vos**. That is exactly how Luis asks in the novel: **¿Cómo te llamás?**

| Usual (tú) | Argentina (vos) |
|---|---|
| tú eres | vos sos |
| ¿Cómo te llamas? | ¿Cómo te llamás? |
| ¿De dónde eres? | ¿De dónde sos? |

At A1 we learn *tú*, which is understood everywhere. But recognising *vos* is very useful, especially if you read our novels in Spanish.
