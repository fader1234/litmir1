## Meet Marcos, the detective from Sinea

This is how the novel «El tren fantasma» begins. Read it — you already understand almost everything:

> **Me llamo Marcos.** **Tengo 35 años**{I'm 35 years old}. **Soy detective**{I'm a detective}. **Vivo**{I live} en Sinea. Sinea es una ciudad bonita.

Now let's imagine that on a night train Marcos meets Natalia, the cardiologist from Moscow.

> — Hola. ¿Cómo te llamas?
> — Me llamo Marcos. ¿Y tú?
> — Natalia. **¿A qué te dedicas?**{What do you do? (for a living)}
> — Soy detective. **Trabajo**{I work} en Sinea. ¿Y tú, **qué haces**{what do you do}?
> — Soy **cardióloga**{cardiologist (female)}. Trabajo en un **hospital**{hospital} en Moscú.
> — **¡Hablas**{You speak} muy bien español!
> — Gracias. **Estudio**{I study} español **todos los días**{every day}. **Leo**{I read} novelas y **escribo**{I write} mucho.
> — **¿Dónde vives?**{Where do you live?}
> — **Vivo**{I live} en Moscú, pero ahora **viajo**{I'm travelling} por Argentina. ¿Y tú?
> — Yo vivo en Sinea. **Busco**{I'm looking for} a mi **novia**{girlfriend}, Alicia.

## 1. What do you do?

- **¿A qué te dedicas?** — What do you do (for a living)?
- **¿Qué haces?** — What are you doing? / What do you do? (depends on the context)
- **Soy detective.** — I'm a detective.

Important: after *ser*, Spanish uses **no article** before a profession. You say *Soy detective*, not «soy un detective» ✘ — unlike English "I'm **a** detective".

## 2. Professions: masculine and feminine

| He | She | English |
|---|---|---|
| médico | médica | doctor |
| cardiólogo | cardióloga | cardiologist |
| profesor | profesora | teacher |
| camarero | camarera | waiter / waitress |
| abogado | abogada | lawyer |
| detective | detective | detective |
| artista | artista | artist |
| periodista | periodista | journalist |
| estudiante | estudiante | student |

The rule is simple: **-o → -a**, consonant **+ a** (*profesor → profesora*). Words ending in **-e** and **-ista** don't change: *el detective, la detective; el artista, la artista*.

## 3. Present tense: regular verbs

Every Spanish verb in the dictionary ends in **-ar**, **-er** or **-ir**. To conjugate a regular verb, drop the ending and add a new one:

| | hablar (to speak) | comer (to eat) | vivir (to live) |
|---|---|---|---|
| yo | habl**o** | com**o** | viv**o** |
| tú | habl**as** | com**es** | viv**es** |
| él / ella / usted | habl**a** | com**e** | viv**e** |
| nosotros / nosotras | habl**amos** | com**emos** | viv**imos** |
| vosotros / vosotras | habl**áis** | com**éis** | viv**ís** |
| ellos / ellas / ustedes | habl**an** | com**en** | viv**en** |

The *yo* form always ends in **-o**: *hablo, como, vivo*. Verbs in *-er* and *-ir* only differ in the *nosotros* and *vosotros* forms.

## 4. Verbs in this unit

| -ar | -er | -ir |
|---|---|---|
| trabajar — to work | leer — to read | vivir — to live |
| estudiar — to study | aprender — to learn | escribir — to write |
| buscar — to look for | comprender — to understand | abrir — to open |
| viajar — to travel | beber — to drink | |
| hablar — to speak | comer — to eat | |

**Marcos busca a Alicia.** When the object is a person, Spanish adds a little **a**: *busco **a** mi novia*. With things, it doesn't: *busco el tren*.

## 5. Negation

To make a sentence negative, put **no** before the verb:

- **No hablo** ruso. — I don't speak Russian.
- Marcos **no vive** en Moscú. — Marcos doesn't live in Moscow.
- — ¿Eres médica? — **No, no soy** médica. Soy cardióloga. (the first *no* means "no", the second "not")

There is no "do / does" in Spanish questions or negations.

## 6. Questions

A Spanish question often differs from a statement only in intonation and the ¿ ? signs:

- Hablas español. — You speak Spanish.
- **¿Hablas español?** — Do you speak Spanish?

Question words carry a written accent: **¿Dónde?** — where? · **¿Qué?** — what? · **¿Cómo?** — how? · **¿Quién?** — who?

## 7. How old are you?

- **¿Cuántos años tienes?** — How old are you?
- **Tengo 35 (treinta y cinco) años.** — I'm 35. Literally: "I have 35 years".

*Tener* ("to have") is irregular — we will study it in a later unit. For now, learn the phrase as a whole.

| 0 cero | 1 uno | 2 dos | 3 tres | 4 cuatro | 5 cinco |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 6 seis | 7 siete | 8 ocho | 9 nueve | 10 diez | 20 veinte |
| 30 treinta | 31 treinta y uno | 35 treinta y cinco | 40 cuarenta | 50 cincuenta | 100 cien |

## 8. The Argentinian way: "vos"

In the novels the characters use **vos** — and with regular verbs the stress moves to the ending:

| tú | vos |
|---|---|
| hablas | hablás |
| comes | comés |
| vives | vivís |
| ¿Qué haces? | ¿Qué hacés? |
| ¿Dónde vives? | ¿Dónde vivís? |
