## The Fader family from Mendoza

In the novel «El misterio de los Fader», Luis brings Natalia from Moscow to Mendoza to meet his father, Sergio. Sergio shows his guest the family album.

> — Mira, Natalia, **esta es mi familia**{this is my family}.
> — ¡Qué bonito! ¿Quién es este hombre?
> — Es **mi padre**{my father}, el **abuelo**{grandfather} de Luis. **Tiene**{he is (lit. has)} noventa años en la foto.
> — ¿Y esta mujer **tan elegante**{so elegant}?
> — Es **mi mujer**{my wife}, **la madre**{the mother} de Luis.
> — Luis, **¿tienes hermanos?**{do you have brothers or sisters?}
> — No, **soy hijo único**{I'm an only child}. Pero **tengo**{I have} muchos **primos**{cousins} en Mendoza.
> — **Nuestra familia**{our family} es grande y un poco **misteriosa**{mysterious}… — dice Sergio, y **sonríe**{smiles}.

Note: **los padres** means "parents" and **los hermanos** means "brothers and sisters". A masculine plural includes both men and women.

## 2. The verb TENER — "to have"

| | TENER |
|---|---|
| yo | **tengo** |
| tú | **tienes** |
| él / ella / usted | **tiene** |
| nosotros / nosotras | **tenemos** |
| vosotros / vosotras | **tenéis** |
| ellos / ellas / ustedes | **tienen** |

It's irregular: **-go** in the *yo* form, and the **e** becomes **ie** in the *tú, él, ellos* forms.

Spanish uses *tener* for age: **Sergio tiene 70 años.** — Sergio is 70 (lit. "has 70 years").

## 3. Possessives: my, your, his…

| Whose? | One thing | Several things |
|---|---|---|
| my | **mi** padre | **mis** padres |
| your | **tu** hermana | **tus** hermanas |
| his / her / your (formal) | **su** hijo | **sus** hijos |
| our | **nuestro** abuelo / **nuestra** abuela | **nuestros** / **nuestras** |
| your (plural, Spain) | **vuestro** / **vuestra** | **vuestros** / **vuestras** |
| their / your (pl.) | **su** familia | **sus** primos |

*Su* can mean "his", "her", "their" or "your (formal)" — context makes it clear. *Tu* (your) has no accent; *tú* (you) does.

## 4. Adjectives: what is he like?

Adjectives usually come **after** the noun and agree in gender and number: *una familia **grande**, unos hombres **altos***.

| He | She | English |
|---|---|---|
| alto | alta | tall |
| bajo | baja | short |
| guapo | guapa | good-looking |
| simpático | simpática | nice, friendly |
| rubio | rubia | blond |
| moreno | morena | dark-haired, tanned |
| inteligente | inteligente | clever |
| joven | joven | young |
| mayor | mayor | old, elderly |

To describe people we use **ser**: **Luis es alto y moreno. Natalia es pelirroja e inteligente.** (Before a word starting with *i-*, *y* becomes **e**.)

## 5. "There is / there are": HAY

**Hay** means "there is / there are". It has a single form for singular and plural:

- En la foto **hay** tres personas. — There are three people in the photo.
- En Mendoza **hay** muchos viñedos. — There are many vineyards in Mendoza.

## 6. The Argentinian way

With *vos*: **vos tenés** (instead of *tú tienes*). **¿Tenés hermanos?**
