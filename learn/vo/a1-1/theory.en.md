## Meet Robert and Natalia

The first chapter of «Föt lätik»: night, rain. Wounded, Robert falls off his motorbike outside a hospital. There he meets Natalia, a doctor. All the sentences below come from the novel!

Hover over a word (or tap it) to see its translation.

> — **Binol malädik?**{Are you ill?} — **vom**{the woman} **äsäkof**{asked}.
> — **Si**{Yes}. **Binob malädik vemo**{I’m very ill}.
> — **No binol malädik vemo**{You’re not very ill}. **Binol man ko nam dolik**{You’re a man with a sore hand}.
> — **Nem obik binon**{My name is} Natalia. **Binob doktor**{I’m a doctor}. **Komol ko ob!**{Come with me!}
> — **Kiom binol?**{Who are you?} — **Binob**{I am} Robert.
> — **No äsäkob nemi olik**{I didn’t ask your name}. **Äsäkob: kiom binol?**{I asked: who are you?}
> — **Binob man ko mot**{I’m a man with a motorbike}.

## 1. Greetings and goodbyes

| Volapük | English |
|---|---|
| Glidis! | Hello! Greetings! |
| Benokömö! | Welcome! |
| Del gudik! | Good day! |
| Neit gudik! | Good night! |
| Adyö! | Goodbye! |
| Danö! | Thank you! |
| Si / No | Yes / No |

In Volapük an adjective comes **after** the noun and ends in **-ik**: *neit gud**ik*** (good night), *dom gret**ik*** (a big house).

## 2. Introducing yourself

- **Kiom binol?** — Who are you?
- **Nem obik binon Natalia.** — My name is Natalia. (Literally: "name my is Natalia".)
- **Binob Robert.** — I’m Robert.
- **Liko stadol? — Stadob gudiko.** — How are you? — I’m well.

## 3. Subject pronouns

| Volapük | English |
|---|---|
| ob | I |
| ol | you |
| om / of | he / she |
| on | it |
| obs | we |
| ols | you (plural) |
| oms / ofs | they (masc. / fem.) |

Possessives add **-ik**: *ob → obik* (my), *ol → olik* (your), *om → omik* (his). Like adjectives, they follow the noun: *nem obik* — my name.

## 4. The verb “binön”

The neatest thing about Volapük: **the pronoun is glued onto the verb**. *bin-* (be) + *ob* (I) = **binob** (I am). So no separate pronoun is needed.

| Person | BINÖN | Example |
|---|---|---|
| ob | **binob** | Binob doktor. |
| ol | **binol** | Binol malädik? |
| om / of | **binom / binof** | Vom binof jönik. |
| on | **binon** | Binon gudik. |
| obs | **binobs** | Binobs frends. |
| oms / ofs | **binoms / binofs** | Solds binoms ko güns. |

Negation is **no** before the verb: *No binol malädik.* The past tense adds the prefix **ä-**: *äbinob* (I was), *äsäkof* (she asked).

## 5. Where are you from?

- **Kiöp lödol?** — Where do you live?
- **Lödob in Rusän.** — I live in Russia.

Countries end in **-än**, and their people in **-änan**: *Rusän* (Russia) → *Rusänan* (a Russian). A woman can be marked with the prefix **ji-**, but usually one form is used.

| He | She | English |
|---|---|---|
| Rusänan | Rusänan | a Russian |
| Deutänan | Deutänan | a German |
| Fransänan | Fransänan | a French person |

## 7. Good to know

Volapük stress is **always on the last syllable**: *vo-la-PÜK, bi-NOB*… except in names. Volapük was created in 1879 by the German priest Johann Martin Schleyer — one of the first constructed languages.
