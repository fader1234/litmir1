## Meet Robert and Katerina

The novel «Le Presentimento»: after the nuclear apocalypse Robert searches for survivors to build «Nostre Utopia». In the snow he finds a woman — Katerina, a Russian.

Hover over a word (or tap it) to see its translation.

> — **Salute**{Hello}! **Io me appella**{My name is} Robert. **Como te appella tu?**{What’s your name?}
> — **Io me appella Katerina**{My name is Katerina}.
> — **Multo placer**{Nice to meet you}, Katerina. **Como sta tu?**{How are you?}
> — **Ben**{Well}, **gratias**{thank you}. **Ma io ha frigido**{But I’m cold}.
> — **De ubi es tu?**{Where are you from?}
> — **Io es de Russia**{I’m from Russia}. **Io es russe**{I’m Russian}. **E tu?**{And you?}
> — **Io es un supervivente**{I’m a survivor}. **Veni con me**{Come with me}!

Interlingua is built from words shared by the Romance languages and English. If you know Spanish, Italian or English, you already understand much of it!

## 2. Introducing yourself

- **Como te appella tu? / Como se appella vos?** — What’s your name? (informal / formal)
- **Io me appella Robert.** — My name is Robert.
- **Multo placer!** — Nice to meet you!
- **Como sta tu? — Ben, gratias.** — How are you? — Fine, thanks.

## 4. The verb “esser”

Good news: Interlingua verbs **don’t change for person**. *Io es, tu es, nos es, illes es* — always the same form **es**.

| Person | ESSER | Example |
|---|---|---|
| io | **es** | Io es Robert. |
| tu | **es** | Tu es russe? |
| ille / illa | **es** | Katerina es russe. |
| nos | **es** | Nos es amicos. |
| vos | **es** | Vos es supervivente? |
| illes | **es** | Illes es in le fortressa. |

A question can start with **esque**: *Esque tu es russe?* — Are you Russian? Negation is **non**: *Io non es russe.*

## 5. Where are you from?

- **De ubi es tu?** — Where are you from?
- **Io es de Russia / de Moscova.** — I’m from Russia / from Moscow.
- **Io vive in le fortressa.** — I live in the fortress.

Nationalities usually don’t change for gender and are written in lower case:

## 7. Good to know

The stress usually falls on the vowel before the last consonant: *ap-PEL-la, sa-LU-te, gra-TI-as*.
