# Chapter 1: The Unarmed

Four years had passed since the Devastation. The long gray, the curtain that had swallowed the sun, had finally torn, but the light it revealed was not one of hope, but of desolation. The world was a scar, and we, the survivors, were the germs festering within it.

For many, venturing to the surface after so long trapped in grimy bunkers was a cause for celebration. I didn't share their enthusiasm. The air, though cleaner, smelled of dust, rust, and things long spoiled. My neighborhood, a district of pre-Fall concrete blocks in a nameless northern city, had always been dismal. Now, with its scorched facades and cracks snaking up the walls like diseased veins, the difference was minimal. "Well, there's not much difference between my neighborhood before and after the apocalypse, haha," I muttered to myself, a dry laugh catching in my throat.

Hunger, a familiar and ferocious wolf, drove me forward. I decided to explore beyond the known limits, venturing into streets I had only seen on faded maps. The old world was a ghost, a story told by those who were losing their memory. In its place, a new ecosystem had emerged: security franchises with patched-up uniforms, charter schools that taught field-stripping a rifle alongside basic math, and small, armed crews controlling every neighborhood with an iron fist. People carried weapons. Many displayed their pistols and rifles with a pathetic pride, as if the cold metal gave them a value they themselves had lost.

I, Vasily—Vasya to the few who still remembered me—was unarmed. My only defense was invisibility.

I was walking with my head down through an unfamiliar district when, turning a corner, I collided head-on with a group of young men. It was a summer night, warm and humid, and they were shirtless, sweaty, and smeared with soot. One, with a glazed look in his eyes, clutched a sawed-off shotgun. Another, bigger, let a chainsaw purr, its gas tank swinging dangerously on his hip.

"Clear the way, damn it!" they shouted in unison, raising their arms and their tools of death. It was less a greeting and more a challenge.

I stood still, paralyzed. I said nothing. I didn't know what to say.

The one with the shotgun frowned. His face darkened. "Hey, what's your problem? You deaf? You don't acknowledge the Crew?"

"I... I didn't know," I stammered, feeling a cold sweat run down my back. "I'm not from around here."

The tension eased a bit, replaced by a mocking condescension. "Ah, a drifter," said the one with the chainsaw, cutting the motor. "In this sector, you acknowledge the Crew. It's a sign of respect. Shows you understand how things work now."

And then, as if reciting a creed, they explained the principles of the "Clear Path"—their philosophy for the new world. They spoke of absolute personal sovereignty, of how every man's will and his property were his only law. "The old systems were a cage," exclaimed the one with the shotgun, fanaticism in his eyes. "We burned the cage! Here, your safety is your business. Your justice is your business. You want something, you trade for it. No appeals, no handouts. It's the purest form of respect. You get what you're strong enough to hold onto."

I nodded, pretending to listen with interest, feigning an understanding I didn't feel. Inside, only one phrase resonated, monotonous and desperate: What a shitty world I've been born into.

After their sermon, they left, roaring into the darkness. I was left alone, with a void in my stomach deeper than the cracks in the asphalt. A few meters ahead, a faint light filtered through the broken window of a building. A sign, crooked and rusty, read "Market." Hope, foolish and persistent, bloomed in my chest.

I pushed the door, and it creaked on its hinges. The interior was a skeleton of empty shelves, smelling of dust and decay. In one corner, behind a counter made of scrap wood, a gaunt man with a dead-eyed stare leveled a hunting rifle at my chest.

"Stop," his voice was flat, devoid of all emotion.

I raised my hands slowly. The wolf of hunger growled in my gut.

"I'm just looking for food," was all I could say, and my voice sounded fragile, like that of a lost child.
