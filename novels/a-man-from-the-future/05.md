### Nina ’ Scafé

At one o'clock, Olga finds Alex waiting outside the café. His cup of water is untouched, and he is turning her card over in his hands. She sits beside him and asks about his morning. This time, Alex has no exciting story to tell.

He explains what happened on Arbat Street. Olga listens without laughing. When he finishes, she puts her bag on the empty chair.

“You frightened her,” she says.

“I know. I thought she didn't understand me.”

“She understood that a stranger wanted her to go somewhere with him. She said no. You needed to stop then.”

Alex nods. He wants to tell Olga about the loneliness of his world, but he knows that it cannot excuse what he did. Svetlana has her own life, and she owes him nothing.

“How do people begin?” he asks. “How do they get to know each other?”

“Usually with a conversation. You ask questions, and you listen to the answers. Sometimes you become friends. Sometimes you don't.”

“There isn't a faster way?”

“There isn't a button, Alex.”

That makes him smile. Olga smiles too, but she waits until he promises to respect people's answers before she changes the subject. Then she asks where he is staying.

“I have a seat in a red machine.”

“That sounds uncomfortable.”

“It is. The people who built it didn't think about sleep.”

Olga takes him inside to meet Aunt Nina. Nina listens to his story about having no money and nowhere to stay. She does not ask him to prove that he comes from the future. She asks whether he knows anything about computers.

The café's old computer is broken, and Alex offers to look at it. He finds a loose cable and repairs the connection. When the screen lights up, Nina gives a satisfied nod.

“You can use the spare room upstairs for a few nights,” she says. “We can discuss the rest tomorrow.”

Alex thanks her. He retrieves his backpack and carries it upstairs, leaving it beside the narrow bed. The room is small, but its window opens onto a courtyard where somebody is playing the piano.

Later, he comes down and asks Olga about her drawings. He wants to know why she draws people reading instead of famous buildings. She tells him that she likes the way people forget about everyone else when a story is good.

Alex listens until she finishes. When he asks another question, it is about her picture. He is beginning to understand what a conversation can be.
