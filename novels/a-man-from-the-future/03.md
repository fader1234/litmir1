### Moscow · 2024

The smell of coffee leads Alex to a small café. He stops outside and watches two friends talking through the window. Their drinks are getting cold, but neither of them seems to care. Alex opens the door.

A woman behind the counter looks up. Her name is Olga; he can read it on her name tag. She has a pencil behind one ear and a little flour on her sleeve.

“Good morning. What would you like?” she asks.

“Coffee, please. And that.” He points to a warm pastry.

Olga puts the pastry on a plate and tells him the price. Alex holds his finger above the payment machine. Nothing happens, so he tries again.

“Do you have a card?” Olga asks.

“My money is here.” He points to a tiny chip under the skin of his finger. “It usually works.”

“Well, it doesn't work here. Do you have any cash?”

Alex searches his pockets, although he knows they are empty. In his world, nobody carries coins or paper money. He looks at the pastry and carefully pushes the plate back.

“I'm sorry. I can't pay.”

Olga studies his face. He seems more confused than dishonest, and he looks very hungry.

“This breakfast is on me,” she says. “But next time, you need rubles.”

“On you?” Alex looks at her clean apron.

“It means I'm paying.”

He thanks her and sits near the counter. The first bite of pastry leaves him silent. In 3024, most meals come from food machines, and they never taste like this.

When the café is quiet, Olga asks where he comes from. Alex tells her the truth. He is from Moscow too, but his Moscow is a thousand years in the future.

“That's a very long trip for breakfast,” she says.

“I came to find love.”

“Then you may need more than coffee.”

Alex laughs, and Olga smiles. She does not believe his story, but she enjoys talking to him. He asks about the drawings on the wall, and she tells him that they are hers. One day, she wants a café with books, pictures, and a place for people to read.

“My aunt Nina owns this café,” she explains. “For now, I work here and draw when it's quiet.”

Before Alex leaves, Olga writes the café's address on a card. She tells him that her shift ends at one. If he still needs help, he can come back then.

Alex leaves his backpack behind the counter with her permission. Outside, he looks at the card again. For the first time in years, someone is expecting him.
