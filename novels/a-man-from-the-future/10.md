### June27 · 2024

The machine lands with a hard bump. Its screen reads eight o'clock, and a message explains that it has moved ten meters to find an empty space. It is hidden behind some empty wooden boxes. Through a gap, Olga sees another red machine beside the wall. She watches Alex leave that machine and walk toward the restaurant without noticing her, then opens the notebook.

It is the same machine at two different moments in its journey. The one beside the wall has not yet taken her to 3024. The one she is standing in has already brought her back. For the next two hours, both are here.

There are also two Olgas. Her earlier self is getting ready for dinner, without any idea what will happen. Olga remembers being that woman. She remembers seeing a face like her own and feeling the room turn around her.

She reads the warning again. The earlier Olga must still make her journey at ten. After that departure, only the returning Olga and her machine will remain. Olga cannot undo the evening that brought her here, but she can finish it.

She locks the machine and walks to the restaurant. At twenty-five past eight, she sees Alex through the window. Relief makes her forget the warning for a moment. She goes inside and takes his arm, wanting to tell him everything.

“Come with me. I need to talk to you.”

Then she sees her earlier self. The other Olga turns pale and falls back into her chair, exactly as she remembers. Alex stays with her, and the waiter brings water. Olga lets go of his arm.

“Wait. I'm sorry. Please stay with her.”

“Who are you?” he asks.

“You'll understand tonight. Wait for me at the station.”

Outside, Olga sits on a low wall. She is shaking, and her sleeve is dirty, but she begins to laugh. For years, people have told her to look at herself from the outside. Now she knows that once is enough.

She waits near the alley, keeping out of sight. A little before ten, she sees her earlier self arrive with Alex's key and notebook. The woman enters the machine beside the wall. A few minutes later, light flashes around it, and it disappears.

Olga checks the alley. One red machine remains, and there is nobody else in the street. The two parts of her journey now fit together. She puts the recorder in her bag and walks quickly to the station.

Alex stands when she enters. He notices the mark on her sleeve and looks into her face. She does not need to tell him which Olga she is.

“You went,” he says.

“Yes. I'm sorry I didn't listen.”

“Are you all right?”

She nods, and he puts down his paper cup. When he holds out his arms, Olga steps into them. For a while, the loud clock is the only thing either of them hears.
