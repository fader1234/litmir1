### June27 · 2024

A week passes. Alex helps Nina with the café's computer and repairs an old radio in the upstairs room. Nina gives him some money for the repairs, and he pays Olga back for his first breakfast. She puts the coins in her pocket with a little bow.

“A thousand years late, but thank you,” she says.

Alex spends his free time exploring the city. In the evenings, he talks to Olga while she puts away her pencils. He learns that she dislikes getting up early, loves old adventure stories, and always forgets where she leaves her keys. She learns that he can fix a screen but cannot fold a paper map.

One afternoon, Olga takes him to a park to meet two friends. They are sitting on a bench, eating cherries from a paper bag. Alex waits while Olga introduces him, then asks if he can sit down.

“Of course,” says one of the women. “Olga says you're interested in history.”

“Very interested. Especially next year.”

The women laugh. Alex starts to explain, then sees Olga smiling and decides to leave it there. Instead, he asks about the book on the bench. Soon they are talking about stories with terrible endings.

When they leave the park, Olga tells him that her friends like him. Alex looks pleased, but he has a different question in mind. It takes him half a street to ask it.

“Would you like to have dinner with me?”

“Do you mean a date?”

“Yes. June twenty-seventh.”

Olga stops walking. For a second, she looks serious, and Alex worries that he has made another mistake. Then she begins to laugh.

“I mean a romantic evening,” she says. “Not the date on the calendar.”

“Oh. Then yes, I mean both.”

They choose a small restaurant near Tverskaya Street for that evening. Alex checks the prices in the window before he agrees. He has enough money for dinner, and this time he knows how to pay.

“You don't have to tell me stories about flying cars,” Olga says. “I like talking to you about ordinary things.”

“But the cars are real.”

“Then tell me about them after dessert.”

At the café, Olga goes back to work and Alex opens the machine's notebook. He wants to understand the old power unit before he shows her the machine. A warning says that, when power is low, the machine can only return to a saved moment. Alex underlines the sentence, then checks the time. For once, the next few hours seem more interesting than the next thousand years.
