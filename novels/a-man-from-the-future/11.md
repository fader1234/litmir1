### Afuturetogether

Outside the station, Olga tells Alex about Pavel and the journey. She gives him the recorder and explains what happened to the two machines. Alex listens carefully, asking questions when he does not understand. They reach the alley long after their dinner should have ended.

Inside the remaining machine, the screen is dark. The return used the last of its power. Alex opens the panel and studies the damaged unit, while Olga stands in the doorway.

“Can you repair it?” she asks.

“Maybe. Not tonight.”

Olga looks toward the street, where someone is closing a shop. She thinks about Nina waiting at the café and the unfinished drawing on her table. Then she turns back to Alex.

“I don't want to live in 3024,” she says. “My home is here.”

“I understand.”

“Do you? You came here to take someone back with you.”

Alex sits on the edge of the seat. For a week, he has imagined returning with a wife, as if another person could complete his plan. Now he thinks about Olga's café, her books, and the people who would miss her.

“I wanted somebody to come into my life,” he says. “I forgot that she would have a life too.”

Olga sits beside him. She admits that traveling without telling him was wrong, even though she wanted to understand him. They talk until a delivery truck arrives at the end of the alley. The driver asks them to close the machine's door so he can get past.

Alex removes the empty power unit. He keeps the recorder and the notebook, but disconnects the controls. Tomorrow, he will arrange to take the machine apart. Tonight, they walk back to the café together. Staying in 2024 does not solve every problem. Alex needs documents, a place of his own, and work he can do without explaining a thousand years of history. He attends appointments, fills out forms, and tells his strange story many times. The recorder helps him show what he cannot explain in words.

Over the following months, his life slowly takes shape. He gets the documents he needs and finds a job repairing computers. Olga begins a small reading club in Nina's café, and Alex builds shelves for the books. Sometimes they argue about ordinary things, including where he leaves his tools.

A year after their first dinner, they return to the same restaurant. Alex orders coffee after dessert and puts a small box on the table. Olga looks at it, then at him.

“It isn't another machine, is it?”

“No. This one only opens.”

Inside is a ring. Alex takes her hand and asks her to marry him. This time, he knows the woman he is asking, and he waits for her answer.

“Yes,” Olga says. “But no time travel on our honeymoon.”

“The machine is gone. We can take a train.”

She laughs and leans across the table to kiss him. Later, they walk home past the café and stop to look at Olga's latest picture in the window. It shows a man trying to fold a map while a woman waits beside him. Alex studies it for a moment, then asks if she can draw the map a little smaller.
