My head was still spinning from Nadia’s sudden kiss and even more sudden exit. I needed a lead, a clue, anything. So, I did the only thing I knew how to do: I went back on the app.

Her name was Susan. Her profile was normal—a little too normal. She liked sunsets, long walks on the beach, and pizza. There was no mention of dead bugs, black widows, or suspiciously timed escapes. Frankly, she seemed boring. And after Veronica and Nadia, boring sounded like a nice, safe change.

We met at a generic café. Susan was nice. She had a friendly smile and wore a bright yellow sweater. She was, in a word, fine.

For the first ten minutes, it was fine. We talked about the weather. The coffee. The scones.

Then she asked, "So, Mark, have you ever had your heart broken?"

And the floodgates opened.

"There was Kevin," she began, sighing dramatically. "He was a musician. A bass player. He left me for his band's groupie. Her name was Sparkle. Can you believe it? Sparkle."

I nodded, trying to look sympathetic. "That's... unfortunate."

"Then there was Derek," she continued, not taking a breath. "He was an accountant. Very stable. But he was allergic to my cat, Mr. Snuffles. He made me choose. I chose Mr. Snuffles, obviously."

"Obviously," I echoed, taking a long sip of my now-cold coffee.

"And don't even get me started on Brian. Brian the vegan chef who tried to replace my leather boots with lettuce. Or Tim, who cried during every movie, even the funny ones. Or—"

She kept going. Name after name. Story after story. I learned about Steve’s weird toe, Greg’s obsession with garden gnomes, and Mike’s fear of buttons. I knew more about these men than I knew about my own brother.

My eyes glazed over. This wasn't a date; it was an audiobook of romantic disappointments. She was definitely not the killer. The only thing she was guilty of was murdering my will to live.

Two hours later, she finally paused to take a breath. "...and that's why I'll never date a man named Gary again. So, Mark," she said, blinking sweetly. "Tell me about your exes."

My mind went blank. I was so deep undercover as "Mark" that I'd almost forgotten my own life. I couldn’t talk about my real exes. And I definitely couldn’t talk about why I was really here.

I just stared at her, my brain completely empty except for the endless list of names she had just recited.

Finally, I broke. The pressure was too much. I leaned forward and said the only thing that felt true in that moment.

"My name's not Mark," I confessed. "It's Frank. And honestly, Susan... the only thing I've ever broken is a case. And maybe a promise. And my brother's favorite record when I was twelve. He never forgave me for that. His name was Tom. He's dead now. Probably murdered by a woman he met online. So... yeah. That's my story."

I sat back, expecting her to scream, or run, or call the police.

Susan just blinked. She took a slow, thoughtful sip of her tea, placed the cup neatly on the saucer, and looked at me with a calm, understanding smile.

"Oh, Frank," she said softly. "That's a classic avoidance technique. I see you're afraid of vulnerability. You know, my ex-boyfriend, Chad, was just like that. He was a therapist, can you believe it? A therapist who was scared of his own feelings! He once…"

I stopped listening. I just paid for both our drinks, gave her a weak wave, and walked out into the night. The killer was still out there, but for the first time, I was more afraid of a woman's dating history.
