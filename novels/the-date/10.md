A week passed. Frank ignored Tom’s calls and messages. He spent his days in his quiet apartment, trying to remember what his life was like before the hunt. It was difficult. The ghost of his brother had been replaced by the very real, very flawed man.

One evening, his doorbell rang. He wasn’t expecting anyone. Cautiously, he looked through the peephole.

It was Susan. The talker. She was holding a small pizza box and wore an apologetic smile.

Frank opened the door. “Susan?”

“Hi, Frank. Not Mark,” she said. “Can I come in? I promise I’ll keep the ex-boyfriend stories to a minimum. I brought pizza as a peace offering.”

He let her in. They sat at his small table, eating in surprisingly comfortable silence for a few minutes.

“I wanted to say sorry,” Susan finally said. “I didn’t know… I mean, Tom told us it was an elaborate prank for his skeptical brother. He made it sound like a fun challenge. We didn’t know about the grief. We didn’t know it was for two years. That wasn’t acting anymore. That was cruelty.”

Frank nodded, swallowing a bite of pizza. “It wasn’t your fault. You were just hired for a role.”

“Still,” she said. “We all feel terrible. Especially Tom. He’s a mess. He’s not an actor; he’s a guy who made a huge, stupid mistake.”

Frank sighed, looking out the window. “I know.”

“He’s outside,” Susan said softly. “In the car. He’s too scared to come up. But he’s here.”

Frank didn’t say anything for a long time. He thought about the brother he’d lost and the brother who had just been given back, in the most confusing way possible.

“Send him up,” Frank said finally.

When Tom walked in, his eyes were red. He looked like he hadn’t slept. No charming smile, no dramatic flair. Just a sorry, scared younger brother.

“I don’t know how to fix this,” Tom whispered.

“You don’t fix it,” Frank said. His voice wasn’t harsh, just tired. “You just… start over. And you never lie to me like that again.”

Tom nodded furiously. “Never. I promise.”

“And you’re coming with me to Mom’s tomorrow,” Frank added. “You’re going to explain everything to her. And you’re going to help her remove that headstone.”

A real, genuine smile broke through Tom’s anxious face for the first time. “Okay. Okay, I will.”

Frank looked at Susan, then back at his brother. The anger was still there, a small, quiet ember, but it was no longer a fire. In its place was a weary understanding, and the faint, fragile beginnings of forgiveness.

His life as a detective hunting a ghost was over. Now, he had something more complicated, and more real: a life with a brother who was foolishly, frustratingly, thankfully alive.

“Now,” Frank said, pushing the pizza box toward Tom. “Are you going to just stand there, or are you going to have some pizza? And for the record, if you ever ask me to analyze a date in a courtroom or tie me to a chair again, I will arrest you myself.”

Tom laughed, a sound of pure relief, and pulled up a chair. The case was closed, not with a dramatic capture, but with a shared meal and a second chance. It was, Frank decided, a much better ending.
