The museum was quiet. Anna and Leo walked past old paintings and old statues. In the middle of the big hall there was a glass box. Inside the box was the turquoise diamond. It was back in its place.

The director, Mr. Bell, was in his office. He was an old man with white hair and small glasses. When he saw Anna, he looked tired and sad.

"I saw the news," he said. "I am very sorry about Charles. He is an honest man."

"Mr. Bell," Anna said. "I need to know about the diamond. Who stole it? What do you know?"

Mr. Bell did not answer. He looked at the window. He looked at the door. He was afraid.

"Please," Anna said. "Charles is in prison. He returned the diamond to you. Now he needs your help."

Mr. Bell took off his glasses. He was quiet for a long time. Then he said:

"First, you must know something. The diamond was not very old. It was not in the museum for a hundred years. It was a gift. A gift to the city, one year ago."

"A gift? From who?"

Mr. Bell looked at her. "From your father, Anna. From the Mayor."

Anna sat down. Her legs felt weak.

"There is more," Mr. Bell said. "A turquoise diamond is very, very rare. In the whole world, there is only one place with turquoise diamonds. Some mines in the north of a small, poor country, very far from here." He stopped. "Charles's country."

Anna's heart beat fast. She remembered Charles's words in the police station: *The slaves worked hard for diamonds.*

"So the diamond came from my father's mines," she said slowly. "From the slaves."

"I think so," Mr. Bell said. "But I am an old man. I had no proof. And I was afraid."

"And the theft?" Leo asked. "Who stole it?"

Mr. Bell opened a drawer in his desk. He took out a small USB stick.

"The museum has cameras," he said. "The night of the theft, the police came and took the video. 'For the investigation,' they said. They never brought it back. But my cameras send a copy to my computer at home. The police did not know that."

"You had a copy all this time?" Anna asked. "Why did you not show it to anyone?"

Mr. Bell's face was red. "Because I saw who was in the video. And I was afraid of them. I am ashamed, Anna. Very ashamed. Yesterday, I saw Charles on TV. A poor immigrant with nothing, and he said the truth to the whole city. An old director with a good job, and I said nothing."

Leo put the USB stick into his laptop. A black-and-white video started.

It was night. The museum hall was dark. A door opened. Two men came in. They had keys. They did not break anything. They turned off the alarm. They walked to the glass box, opened it and took the diamond.

One man looked up at the camera for one second.

"It is them," Leo said. "The two policemen. The same policemen who arrested Charles today."

But the video did not finish there. The second camera was outside, on the street. The policemen got into a car. The car drove one block and stopped next to a hole in the road. The hole had a sign: ROAD WORK – NEW ASPHALT TOMORROW. One policeman put something small into the hole. He covered it with dirt.

"They put the diamond under the road," Anna said. "Tomorrow there was going to be new asphalt on top. Nobody was going to find it. Ever."

"And on the radio, the Mayor said: 'The thief is an immigrant,'" Leo said. "The police searched the immigrant neighborhoods. All the people in Sinea were afraid of immigrants. And three weeks later, there was an election."

"But then a poor immigrant worker dug a hole in that road," Anna said. "And he found the diamond. And he gave it back."

For a moment, nobody spoke.

"This video is very important," Mr. Bell said. "But you cannot give it to the city police. They work for your father."

"I know," said Anna. "There is the special police. The national police. They are not from Sinea. My father cannot control them."

She put the USB stick in her pocket. Then she stood up.

"But first," she said, "I must talk to my father."
