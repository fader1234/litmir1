*One year later.*

It was election day again in Sinea. And again, the sun was shining.

This time, there were no hackers and no false papers. Charles had real papers in his pocket. He was a legal resident of Sinea. He was a candidate like everybody else.

The other candidate was a young woman, a doctor. She was honest and intelligent. Charles liked her. In their debate on TV, nobody said anything bad about immigrants. They talked about schools, hospitals and buses.

In the evening, they counted the votes. It was close. Very close. But in the end, Charles won.

The doctor walked to him and shook his hand. "Congratulations, Mr. Mayor. Do your best."

"Thank you," Charles said. "And can you help me? I am going to need good people. I want to open a free hospital for workers."

She smiled. "Yes. I would like that."

* * *

On his first morning as Mayor, Charles did not go to City Hall by car. He took the bus.

The bus was new and clean. And it was free. Everybody could get on: students, old people, workers, mothers with children. A small girl looked at him and said to her mother, "Mama, is that the Mayor? On the bus?"

Charles got off the bus at the port.

A ship was arriving. People were coming down from it with bags and boxes. Some looked happy. Some looked tired. Many looked lost.

One young man stood alone next to the water. He had a small bag in his hand. He looked around. He did not know where to go.

Charles knew that face very well. He saw it every day in the mirror, one year ago.

Then two police officers, a man and a woman, walked to the young man. Charles stopped. His heart beat fast. Old memories.

"Hello," the policeman said to the young man. "Welcome to Sinea. Are you lost? Do you need help?"

The young man was surprised. "I… I have no papers," he said quietly.

"That is okay," the policewoman said. "You are not in trouble. Go to the Welcome House. It is on the main square. They can help you with papers, a bed and a job. It is free." She pointed. "And you can take the bus. The bus is free too."

The young man smiled for the first time. "Thank you," he said.

Charles smiled too. He walked to the young man.

"Hello. My name is Charles," he said. "I am going to the main square too. Can I show you the way?"

* * *

In the afternoon, Charles went to Anna's school. The children were in the computer room with their teacher, Leo. Leo had a job now. A real job. He loved it. "The children are very good with computers," he said. "Maybe one day they will be hackers too. But good hackers!"

Mr. Bell visited the school too. He told the children the story of the turquoise diamond. At the end, he always said, "And do you know the most important thing? Not the diamond. The honest man who gave it back."

Some things were still difficult. Martin was in prison. Every month, Anna went to visit him. For many months, he did not want to talk. But last week, he gave her a letter. It was very short. It said: *You were right. I am sorry. Please say thank you to him.*

Anna gave the letter to Charles. He read it two times. Then he put it in his pocket, next to his papers.

* * *

In the evening, Charles and Anna walked to the port. The sun was going down. The water was red and gold.

"Do you remember?" Anna said. "You stopped me in the street. You were lost. You asked me about a good place to sleep."

"And you sent me to the worst hotel in the city," Charles said.

Anna laughed. "I did not know! I am sorry!"

Charles stopped walking. He took a small box out of his pocket. He opened it. Inside, there was a simple silver ring. No diamond.

"I think I have seen enough diamonds for my whole life," he said. "Anna, you helped me when I was nobody. You chose the truth when it was very hard. You are the bravest person I know." He took a breath. "Will you marry me?"

Anna looked at the ring. Then she looked at him. Her eyes were full of tears, but this time they were happy tears.

"Yes," she said. "Yes, Charles!"

Behind them, a new ship was arriving at the port. On it, there were new people with small bags, tired and hungry and afraid.

But now, in Sinea, there was someone waiting for them.

**THE END**
