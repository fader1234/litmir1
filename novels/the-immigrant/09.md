The next morning, Charles woke up early. He could not believe it. He was the new Mayor of Sinea!

He walked to City Hall. The sun was shining. People in the street smiled at him. A bus driver waved. "Good morning, Mr. Mayor!" he shouted.

Charles sat at the big desk in the Mayor's office. It was the same office from the bad day with Martin. But now the room felt different. Anna brought two cups of coffee.

"So, Mr. Mayor," she said, smiling. "What is your first job?"

"The free buses," Charles said. "I promised. A promise is a promise."

They were working on the bus plan when Leo ran into the office. His face was white.

"Charles! Anna! Turn on the TV! Now!"

Anna turned on the TV. Martin was on the news. He was standing in front of his big house. He was not angry now. He was calm. And that was more dangerous.

"People of Sinea," Martin said. "You have a new Mayor. But do you know the truth about him? His papers are not real! A hacker made them on a computer. Your new Mayor is a liar. He is illegal. And an illegal man cannot be the Mayor of Sinea!"

Then Martin showed a paper to the camera. "Here is the proof!"

Charles turned off the TV. The room was very quiet.

"How does he know?" Anna asked.

Leo looked at the floor. "My computer… Somebody looked at my computer last night. I think it was Martin's people. I am sorry. I am so sorry."

Charles stood up. He walked to the window. He looked at the city: the streets, the buses, the people. For a long time, he did not say anything.

"Charles," Anna said quietly. "We can say it is not true. We can say Martin is lying. People will believe you. They love you."

Charles turned around.

"No, Anna," he said. "Martin is not lying this time. My papers are not real. That is the truth. I said to the people: honesty and hope. I cannot start with a lie."

Just then, there was a loud knock on the door. Two policemen came in.

Charles knew them. They were the two policemen from his first night in Sinea. The policemen who took his money.

The first policeman smiled. It was not a nice smile. "Hello again, Charles. No papers? That is a big problem."

"Yes," Charles said. "It is a problem. And it is my problem. I made a mistake."

He gave them his hands. The policeman put handcuffs on him.

"Charles, no!" Anna cried.

"Anna," Charles said. His voice was calm. "Martin wants me to run away. I am not going to run away again. Not this time."

The policemen took Charles out of City Hall. Outside, there were many journalists with cameras. They were shouting questions.

"Mr. Mayor! Are your papers false?"

Charles stopped. He looked at the cameras.

"Yes," he said. "My papers are false. I was wrong. I am sorry. But I want to say one more thing to the people of Sinea: the truth about the diamond is not finished. Ask who stole it. Ask why."

The policemen pushed him into the police car.

Anna stood in front of City Hall. She was crying. But then she wiped her eyes. She remembered Charles's words: *Ask who stole it. Ask why.*

"Leo," she said. "Come with me. We are going to the museum."
