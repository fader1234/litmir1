Charles went back to his small hotel room. He felt more afraid than ever. He took the diamond out of his pocket. It shone brightly, even in the dark room. It was beautiful, but now it felt dangerous.

He turned on the small television. The Mayor of Sinea was on the news. The Mayor's face was serious.

"People of Sinea," the Mayor said. "A precious diamond, part of our history, was stolen from our museum. We have information. We believe the thief is an immigrant. The police will search every immigrant neighborhood. We will find this criminal!"

Charles turned off the TV. His hands were cold. He felt sick. The Mayor was talking about him! The police were not just looking for a diamond; they were looking for an immigrant. He was the perfect suspect.

He looked at the diamond in his hand. He had two choices.

His first idea was to sell the diamond. It could solve all his money problems. He could get a lot of cash, leave Sinea, and start a new life in another city. He would not be poor anymore. But it was a big risk. How could he sell it? Who could he trust? If the police caught him with the diamond, his life would be over.

His second idea was to give the diamond back. But how? If he went to the police, would they believe his story? After his first meeting with the police, he knew they were corrupt. They would probably take the diamond and still arrest him. He had no papers. He was an immigrant. No one would believe him.

He sat on his bed for a long time, thinking. He thought about the kind woman who helped him. He thought about his dream of a better life. He didn't want to be a thief. He wanted to be an honest man.

Finally, he made his decision. He knew it was the right thing to do.
