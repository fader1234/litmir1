It was evening when Anna arrived at her father's house. The lights were on. The door was open.

Martin was in his bedroom. There was a big suitcase on the bed. He was putting clothes into it very fast.

"Papa," Anna said. "Where are you going?"

Martin did not look at her. "On a trip. For business. A few weeks."

"Tonight? The day after the election?"

Martin stopped. He looked at his daughter. "What do you want, Anna?"

Anna took out her phone. She showed him the video from the museum: the dark hall, the two policemen, the glass box, the hole in the road.

Martin watched the video. His face did not change. But his hands closed into fists.

"Where did you get this?"

"It does not matter. Is it true, Papa? The mines? The slaves? You stole your own diamond and said, 'The thief is an immigrant'?"

For a long moment, Martin did not answer. Then he sat down on the bed, next to the suitcase. Suddenly, he looked old.

"You do not understand," he said. "When I was young, I had nothing. Nothing! The mines made us rich. This house, your school, your beautiful clothes, your trips… Everything came from the mines."

"From the slaves," Anna said.

"From hard work."

"Not *your* hard work, Papa."

Martin stood up. His voice became angry. "And the diamond? It was politics! People need a simple story before an election. 'The immigrants are dangerous. Only Martin can protect you.' It always works. It was not my plan to hurt anyone."

"You hurt Charles," Anna said. "You hurt all the immigrants in this city. And the people in your mines… you hurt them every day."

Martin walked to her. He spoke more quietly now, like a nice father.

"Anna, listen to me. Give me the video. I can make one phone call. Charles goes free tonight. He gets on a ship and goes to another country. Nobody goes to prison. You and I stay a family. Everybody is happy."

Anna looked at the phone in her hand. For one second, she wanted to say yes. It was easy. Charles free. Her father safe. No more problems.

Then she remembered Charles in front of City Hall, with the handcuffs on his hands, looking at the cameras. *I am not going to run away again.*

"No, Papa," she said. "Charles does not want to run away. And the people in the mines cannot run away. Somebody must help them."

"You are my daughter!"

"Yes," Anna said. Her eyes were full of tears. "That is why I am telling you this first. Go to the special police. Tonight. Tell them everything: the mines, the diamonds, the policemen. It is your last chance to be an honest man."

Martin looked at her for a long time. Then he closed the suitcase.

"My ship leaves at midnight," he said. "Goodbye, Anna."

He took the suitcase and walked out of the house. A black car was waiting for him. It drove away.

Anna stood alone in the empty bedroom. Her hands were shaking. She took a small card out of her pocket. Mr. Bell gave it to her at the museum: *Inspector Rosa Vega – National Special Police.*

She called the number.

"Inspector Vega? My name is Anna. I have a video about the museum diamond. And my father… my father is going to the port. His ship leaves at midnight."

* * *

The port at night was dark and cold. There was fog on the water. A big ship was waiting. Its lights were yellow in the fog.

It was the same port where Charles arrived in Sinea, with nothing, on his first day.

Martin walked fast to the ship. He had his suitcase in one hand and a ticket in the other. He was almost there. Only ten more steps.

Then many lights turned on. Blue lights. Police cars came from every direction.

"Stop! National Special Police!"

A woman in a dark coat walked to Martin. She was not tall, but she had a strong voice.

"Mr. Martin, I am Inspector Rosa Vega. You must come with me."

Martin looked around. There was no place to run. Then he saw a person behind the police cars. It was Anna.

"You did this?" he asked her. His voice was very quiet.

Anna walked to him. She was crying, but her voice was clear.

"No, Papa. You did this. A long time ago."

Inspector Vega put handcuffs on Martin. The police took him to a car. Before he got in, Martin looked back at his daughter one more time. Then the door closed.

Inspector Vega came to Anna. She put a hand on her shoulder.

"That was very brave," she said. "It is the most difficult thing a person can do."

"What happens now?" Anna asked.

"Now my team goes to the city police station. Two policemen must answer many questions." Inspector Vega smiled a little. "And there is a man there in a cell. He must not sleep in prison tonight."
