# Writing, Love and the Dream of a Better World

*An interview with writer Luis Fader — October 20, 2026*

**1. You are a writer and a language teacher. When did you start writing, and what does literature mean to you?**

My relationship with literature began when I was a child. I used to invent stories in my mind, but I never imagined that one day I would become a writer.

When I was twelve, I started drawing comics and creating my own stories. In a way, I was already a young writer. At about fifteen, I wrote my first novel, or something like a novel. It was called *The Blue God*. It was about a blue cat that sat on different buildings and protected people at night. I do not remember the whole story now!

Later, I began studying languages and teaching them. I wanted to share my love of stories with my students. For example, I wrote *A Man from the Future* because I wanted to make teaching and learning English more enjoyable.

**2. What feelings or personal ideas often appear in your novels?**

There is always a humanist idea in my stories: the dream of a better world. Sometimes I criticize the world we live in. Sometimes I imagine people trying to build a new society.

Of course, this new world also has problems and contradictions. But my main idea never changes. I believe we need to imagine a better world and try to create it.

**3. Love is important in your stories. What do you want to show when you write about it?**

When I write about love, I often show the love of a man for a woman. I write from a man's point of view because it is something I know from my own experience.

I think our world has lost a lot of its romantic spirit. There is very little romance today. In my novels, I want to show that romance is one of the most beautiful parts of human life.

**4. Your novels also include struggles for power and difficult decisions. Is there a personal conflict behind these stories?**

Yes. My biggest personal conflict is that I know we live in an imperfect world. I have ideas about how to make it better, but I also have physical and material limits. I cannot change everything in real life.

In literature, I can explore the changes I would like to see. Writing is also a safer way to do this. If I decided to dedicate my whole life to changing the real world, I would have to leave many things behind, including my art.

I do not want to stop writing. I prefer to share my ideas through stories and let readers think about them.

**5. If you could express the main idea of your work in one sentence, what would it be?**

I have said this in other interviews, and I still believe it today:

**“Love is magic, and magic is love.”**
