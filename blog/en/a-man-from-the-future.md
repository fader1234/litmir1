# A Man from the Future: travelling a thousand years to learn how to love

**Moscow, 3024. The computers can answer almost any question, but they cannot tell Alex how to stop feeling lonely.** Twenty years ago, all the women and girls in his world disappeared. Nobody knows why. Alex still remembers his mother singing in the kitchen, but he cannot remember the whole song.

Then, behind some empty boxes in an old workroom, he finds a red machine with two words above the door: TIME MACHINE.

This is how *A Man from the Future* by Luis Fader begins. It is a short, funny and surprisingly tender novel — and it is also an easy reader for anyone who is learning English.

## A world without women, a city without songs

The idea is simple and a little sad. In Alex's Moscow there are no children, and the city grows quieter every year. When he reads about busy streets, music and people sitting together in cafés, he understands that the world he dreams about still exists — a thousand years ago.

So Alex packs a small backpack and travels to Moscow in 2024. There, even a cup of coffee is an adventure. He doesn't have money, documents or any idea how people talk to each other. He only has a plan: find a wife and take her home to the future.

## "Come with me to the future, baby"

Of course, the plan goes wrong. On Arbat Street, Alex learns a line from an old romantic movie and tries it on a stranger. In the movie, the woman smiles. In real life, she is afraid.

It is one of the most important moments in the book. A bookseller stands at the door and says four simple words: *"You need to listen."* Alex stops, looks at her face and finally understands. He apologises and walks away.

The novel is a comedy, but here it says something serious: love is not a prize you can win with the right sentence. It begins when you stop talking and start listening.

## Olga, and two Olgas

Then Alex meets Olga. She has a café, books, friends and a drawing she hasn't finished. Their first evening together is full of small accidents — a piece of bread that escapes from a plate, a question about documents that Alex cannot answer — and the reader starts to hope.

And then the time machine makes everything wonderfully complicated. A woman in a blue dress comes into the restaurant… and she looks exactly like Olga. We won't tell you more. Just remember the warning in the old notebook: *returning to a time before you leave can create a meeting with your earlier self.*

## The real journey

The best line of the book comes near the end, when Alex finally understands his mistake:

> "I wanted somebody to come into my life. I forgot that she would have a life too."

That is the real journey of *A Man from the Future*. Not from 3024 to 2024, but from loneliness to respect. Alex arrives in the past wanting to take someone away. He learns that loving a person means making room in your life for theirs.

## A book to read — and a book to learn with

*A Man from the Future* is written mostly in the Present Simple, with short chapters and clear, natural English. Every chapter has a vocabulary list with American pronunciation and exercises that slowly introduce new grammar. For Russian speakers, there is also a full Russian translation, an answer key and even a map of Olga's journey through time — because time travel is confusing, even in simple English.

It is a perfect first novel in English: easy enough to finish, and interesting enough that you want to.
