# Writing to Tell the World I Exist: Five Questions for Luis Fader

*Entrevista a Robert Fader · 21/10/2026*

**1. What does being a writer mean to you?**

It is a need to tell the world not only that I exist, but that I am an important part of it. Writing gives a voice to my restless soul.

**2. When did writing become more than a hobby?**

When I began seeing the reactions of the people who gradually became my readers. Their enthusiasm showed me that writing could no longer be mere entertainment: it had become something deeper.

**3. Where do your stories come from?**

It is much like composing music, which I also do. Ideas arrive on their own or arise through encounters and cultural clashes. In The Ghost Train, there are absurd carriages: a swimming-pool car, a carriage where everything costs two hundred rubles, and a confessional carriage where you must confess your sins—and pay—to pass through. Dreams, unusual people and funny situations all feed my imagination. I do not brainstorm; ideas come and stay until I put them through the filter of writing.

**4. What do you hope readers take away?**

I want them to leave with a good impression, optimism and positive emotions. Above all, I want them to feel that the world can be better.

**5. What literary dream do you still want to fulfill?**

I would love to print a run of my books and see them for sale on a real bookshop shelf. My happiest moment would be to happen to be there when someone buys one, unaware that the author is standing nearby.
